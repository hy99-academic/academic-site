I study how science teachers notice and teach science and engineering practices, and how teachers, youth, and communities can design science learning around real-world problems like hurricanes. Across both lines of work, I am interested in how teachers learn: through looking closely at classroom practice, and through designing learning experiences together.

## Science Teacher Noticing of Science and Engineering Practices

Teacher noticing, what teachers attend to, how they interpret it, and how they respond, is a core dimension of teaching expertise. Yet the noticing of *science-specific* practices remains underexplored, particularly for teachers working within reform-based curriculum such as the NGSS.

{{< callout title="My doctoral and ongoing work investigates" >}}
- What preservice and in-service teachers attend to, and how they interpret events, in science classroom videos
- How teachers' interpretations of Science and Engineering Practices (SEPs) relate to their own classroom enactment
- How emotions influence what teachers notice — and what they overlook
{{< /callout >}}

Across this work, I view noticing not only as a cognitive practice but also as a relational one: what teachers attend to shapes whose contributions are taken up as substantive in the classroom.

I am also interested in how professional development, including video-based approaches, can help preservice and in-service teachers learn to notice and build on students' thinking.

## Co-Designing Science and Engineering Learning for Real-World Problems

I partner with teachers, youth, and community members to design science and engineering learning around real-world problems such as hurricanes and local environmental issues. I see co-design both as a way to create learning experiences rooted in students' places and communities, and as a powerful setting for teacher learning.
